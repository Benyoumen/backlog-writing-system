# Frame: follow-up after customer meetings

> Fictional example. Acme is not a real company; every person, number and date is illustrative.

Date: 2026-02-16 | Author: Product manager | Sponsor: VP Sales | Decision: proceed to discovery (one prototype week)

## The problem, in the person's words

> "I get back to the office at seven and still have four follow-ups to write. By the time I open the CRM I have forgotten half of what they said."

Account executive, field sales, interview on 2026-02-11. Four of the five reps interviewed said a version of the same thing.

## Who has it and how often

Account executives, 38 at Acme (illustrative), three to five customer meetings a day each. The sales manager sees the effect every week, when the forecast is read against records older than the last meeting.

## Evidence

Held:
- 41 percent of opportunities were updated within one working day of their last meeting (CRM timestamps, January 2026, all reps; illustrative).
- "No follow-up yet" was the most frequent reason given for a deal going quiet in the weekly pipeline review, four weeks out of four (sales manager's notes, January 2026).

Missing:
- How long a follow-up email takes to write today. Ask five reps to time it this week.
- Whether late follow-ups lose deals or only annoy. The CRM shows a correlation, not a cause. Discovery will ask three customers who went quiet, if the sales manager agrees.

## Outcome and the number

| Outcome | Baseline | Target | By | Measured from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follow-ups for meetings with notes sent within 2 hours of the meeting end | To measure in the two weeks before launch; assumed next business day until then | 80 percent | 60 days after launch | The assistant's trail: meeting end against sent-email timestamp |
| Opportunities with a CRM update after their last meeting | 41 percent (January 2026) | 90 percent | 60 days after launch | CRM timestamps |

## Appetite

Six weeks of two people for a first usable release, decided with the VP Sales on 2026-02-13. If the first release does not fit, scope is cut; the six weeks are not extended.

## Not doing

- Customer success meetings: their notes follow another format.
- Scheduling the next meeting: calendar write access is not agreed.
- Quotes and pricing changes.
- Phone meetings without notes.
- Languages other than English in the first release.

## What was proposed

The request arrived as "an AI assistant that sends the follow-up and updates the CRM automatically". Recorded and set aside. Whether anything is sent or written without the rep is a spec decision, not a frame decision.

## Gate sentence

"We want 80 percent of follow-ups out within 2 hours of the meeting, within 60 days of launch, and we will spend six weeks of two people to find out."
