4. Items by epic

The plain sentence is always visible. The numbered sections open on demand.

Epic 1

Follow-up after customer meetings

After a customer meeting, the rep reviews a drafted follow-up and a proposed CRM update instead of writing both from scratch.

The numbered sections
  1. Why it exists. Late follow-ups are the most common reason deals go quiet, and reps lose time writing the same email and CRM note after every meeting.
  2. Goal. Reps send a reviewed follow-up quickly, and the CRM stays current without manual entry.
  3. Metrics.
    • Share of follow-ups sent within 2 hours of the meeting end.
    • Share of finished meetings with a current CRM update.
    • Share of drafts the rep sends without rewriting the facts.
  4. In scope.
    • Finished meeting list
    • Draft
    • Send
    • CRM preview
    • CRM apply
    • Manager view
  5. Out of scope.
    • Customer success meetings
    • Scheduling the next meeting
    • Editing another rep's records
  6. Risks.
    • Risk: drafts contain unsourced facts. Mitigation: eval set with a 100 percent pass mark on never-cases before phase 1 exit.
    • Risk: the CRM changes between preview and apply. Mitigation: re-read before write and stop on change.
    • Risk: reps ignore the list. Mitigation: show it as the first screen they open.

F1.1

Meetings and notes

The rep sees which meetings just ended and what was noted in each.

The numbered sections
  1. Behavior.
    • List finished meetings of the day.
    • Show end time and attendees.
    • Show a note excerpt.
    • Refresh on open.
  2. Acceptance.
    • A finished meeting appears within 15 minutes of its end (finding A2).
    • A meeting with no notes is marked as such.

US01

Show today's finished meetings with their notes Phase 1, size M, 3 days, days 1 to 3

Each morning and after each meeting, the rep sees what happened in their meetings without opening three tools.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want to see my finished meetings with their notes so that I can review them in one place.
  2. Why it exists. Without this list the assistant has nothing to draft from, and the rep keeps switching tools.
  3. Demo. The rep opens the list and sees today's meetings with notes.
  4. Acceptance.
    • Each finished meeting appears with end time, attendees and a note excerpt.
    • A meeting without notes is flagged.
    • Only the rep's own meetings appear.
  5. Exceptions.
    • Calendar unreachable: show the last good list with its timestamp.
  6. Traceability. Spec, capability meeting-intake.

F1.2

Follow-up draft and send

The rep gets a drafted follow-up, edits it and sends it from their own mailbox.

The numbered sections
  1. Behavior.
    • Draft from notes.
    • Show the facts used.
    • Edit.
    • Send from the rep's mailbox.
  2. Acceptance.
    • No fact outside the notes and the CRM.
    • Send only after an explicit rep action.

US02

Draft a follow-up email from the meeting notes Phase 1, size M, 3 days, days 4 to 6, after US01

After a meeting, the rep finds a ready draft of the follow-up email with the facts it used listed beside it.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want a drafted follow-up so that I review instead of write.
  2. Why it exists. The draft is the main time saving, and listing the facts is how the rep trusts it.
  3. Demo. Open a finished meeting and read its draft with the sources shown.
  4. Acceptance.
    • A draft exists for each meeting with notes.
    • Each fact is listed with its source.
    • No fact has no source.
  5. Exceptions.
    • Notes too short: show a message instead of a draft.
  6. Traceability. Spec, capability follow-up-draft. Eval cases E01 to E06.

US03

Send the follow-up after the rep edits it Phase 2, size M, 3 days, days 7 to 9, after US02

The rep edits the draft and sends it, and nothing is ever sent without that action.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want to send the follow-up from my own mailbox so that the customer hears from me.
  2. Why it exists. This is the one action that reaches the customer, so it is the autonomy boundary that matters most.
  3. Demo. Edit a draft, send it, and see it in the sent folder.
  4. Acceptance.
    • Send runs only after an explicit rep action.
    • The email leaves the rep's mailbox.
    • The sent email id is stored in the trail.
  5. Exceptions.
    • Mailbox error: keep the draft and show the error.
  6. Traceability. Spec, autonomy boundary "Ask first: send an email".

F1.3

CRM write-back

The assistant proposes the CRM update, shows it, and applies it only when the rep approves.

The numbered sections
  1. Behavior.
    • Build a preview.
    • Show old and new values with sources.
    • Apply after approval.
    • Stop if the record changed.
  2. Acceptance.
    • No write without approval.
    • Every write re-reads the record first.

US04

Show a CRM update preview for a finished meeting Phase 1, size M, 3 days, days 4 to 6, after US01

After a meeting, the rep sees exactly which CRM fields would change and why.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want a CRM update preview so that I can approve changes quickly.
  2. Why it exists. The preview is what makes a write safe, and without it reps would not allow writes.
  3. Demo. Open a finished meeting and see each field with old and new values.
  4. Acceptance.
    • Each field shows old value, new value and source.
    • Nothing is written.
  5. Exceptions.
    • Record locked: show a message.
  6. Traceability. Spec, capability crm-writeback.

US05

Apply the CRM update the rep approved Phase 2, size L, 5 days, days 7 to 11, after US04

When the rep approves a preview, the CRM is updated, and if the record changed in the meantime the update stops.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want my approved update applied so that I do not retype it.
  2. Why it exists. This closes the loop and proves the autonomy contract holds when the system writes.
  3. Demo. Approve a preview and see the CRM change.
  4. Acceptance.
    • Apply runs only after approval.
    • The record is re-read before the write.
    • The apply stops if the record changed since the preview.
    • The result is recorded in the trail.
  5. Exceptions.
    • CRM unavailable: keep the preview and retry later.
  6. Traceability. Spec, autonomy boundary "Ask first: write any field to the CRM". Built through the single write path described in the agent brief.

US06

Block the update when the contact has no CRM match Phase 2, size S, 2 days, days 10 to 11, after US04

If a meeting attendee is not a contact on the deal, the assistant does not propose an update and says why.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As an account executive, I want the assistant to refuse unmatched contacts so that it never changes the wrong record.
  2. Why it exists. Writing to the wrong deal is the failure reps fear most.
  3. Demo. Open a meeting with an unknown attendee and see the reason shown.
  4. Acceptance.
    • With no matching contact on the deal, no preview is offered.
    • The reason is shown.
  5. Exceptions.
    • More than one possible match: ask the rep to choose.
  6. Traceability. Spec, autonomy boundary "Never: contact anyone who is not already a contact on the deal".

F1.4

Activity trail

The manager sees what the assistant suggested and what the reps did with it.

The numbered sections
  1. Behavior.
    • Record every suggestion, send, apply and ignore with a timestamp.
    • Show counts per rep per week.
  2. Acceptance.
    • Counts reconcile with the trail for one week of test data.

US07

Show the manager what was sent, applied and ignored Phase 3, size S, 2 days, days 12 to 13, after US03, US05

The sales manager sees per rep, per week, how many suggestions were sent, applied or ignored.

The numbered sections
  1. Statement. As a sales manager, I want a weekly activity view so that I can coach follow-up habits.
  2. Why it exists. It is the measurement of the success criterion and the source for coaching.
  3. Demo. Pick a week and see counts per rep.
  4. Acceptance.
    • Counts per rep for sent, applied and ignored.
    • Counts reconcile with the trail.
  5. Exceptions.
    • A week with no data shows zero, not an error.
  6. Traceability. Spec, capability activity-trail.

9. In the tracker

The order of creation, so links and iterations exist when the stories do.

The epic, then Features F1.1 to F1.4, then US01 to US07, then the dependency links, then the iteration assignment by phase. The tracker assigns its own codes at creation; the mapping from these ids to tracker codes is kept in the numbering register.

Gates run before generating this page

  • Story days per epic match the budget (21)
  • Story ids sequential from US01 to US07
  • Every story has a phase, a plain sentence and its sections
  • No dependency points to a later phase, an unknown id, or a cycle
  • Every phase has a story and an exit gate
  • Dates derived from the start date 2026-03-09, never typed
